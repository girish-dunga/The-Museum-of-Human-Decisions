Exhibit 2.1

The patient

Tuesday, 07:42 · fourth attempt

Ravi has called the clinic three times before work. The hold music is the only thing that answers. He doesn’t want an app. He wants to know whether anyone can see him this week.

The decision

Show real availability first, before asking who you are. Booking became three taps, and the morning phone queue fell by 41%.

Exhibit 2.2

The parent

Thursday, 02:10 · day three of antibiotics

Priya is standing in a dark kitchen with a syringe of medicine. Did Sam give the 8 o’clock dose? The note on the fridge doesn’t say. A double dose is worse than a missed one.

The decision

Design for the household, not the individual. One shared timeline that answers a single question at 2am: has it been given?

Exhibit 2.3

The commuter

Monday, 07:58 · 90 seconds to decide

Tom is walking fast, coffee in one hand, phone in the other. The platform has changed. He has one glance, maybe two, before he chooses a staircase.

The decision

Remove everything that isn’t the next action. One number, one instruction, one countdown. Fewer screens, read faster.

Sunday, 23:16 · Flood claim

Upload the photos of the damage

Tap upload to see what Grace sees, three times.

Exhibit 2.4

The person under stress

Insurance claim, after a flood

Grace has photographed every ruined room. The upload fails for the third time. Stress narrows attention, so “something went wrong” reads as “you did something wrong.”

The decision

Errors should lower someone’s heart rate. Say what is safe first, then what happened, then exactly one next step, and a human name.

Layer 02

Design decisions

Each decision looks different depending on who is standing in front of it. Choose a point of view.

Show availability before login

A slot is visible in under a minute, before anyone asks for personal details.

Reminder the evening before

Remembers the appointment for you. Too many reminders would feel like nagging, so we send one.

One shared household timeline

Two adults can see the same list, so nobody has to guess whether a dose was given.

Layer 04

Failed experiments

Retired with notes, not shame. Open each one to see why it did not survive.

Book by chatbot, v2

Three of eight participants gave up partway through the conversation. Retired within a week.

What survived: the question “what do you need?” became a plain form.

Reminder upsell on the confirmation screen

A paid reminder upgrade, shown at the moment of relief. Removed before launch. I argued for it, and I was wrong.

Lesson: never sell at the point where someone has just finished something hard.
